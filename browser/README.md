# Squish Pocket — browser prototype

An independent physics merge game inspired by Sobosuba, using original code and graphics. Merge matching numbers up to **4096** and keep settled blobs below the dotted line.

The preparation area holds three flat, deformable jelly blobs in a row. Tap any one to select it. Pull a blob downward to build shot force, then release to launch it from its own place in the row, not from the middle. Downward distance controls force: a short pull is gentle and a 180-unit pull reaches full force. A pull counts as charging when it moves down more than 4 units and its downward part exceeds 35% of its sideways part, so diagonal pulls toward the bottle charge too. The aiming guide appears as soon as charging starts, and the aim angle stays steady during the first 24 units of pull. Return to the starting height or cancel to stop; lifting above the starting point changes the gesture into preparation mode. The blob visibly stretches while charging, the force meter and guide update, and charging cannot cause a preparation merge.

Lift a blob upward to merge or rearrange it. Move across the preparation row and neighboring blobs slide into the vacated slots; release near the row to insert it in the highlighted position. Moving the middle blob to the right pushes the old right blob into the middle. Moving an end blob to the other end shifts both remaining blobs in order. Matching targets stay still so a lifted blob can contact them for synthesis. A lifted gesture never becomes a shot when lowered again. Releases far from the row return around the glass under the invisible attraction. Preparation bodies cannot be placed directly inside the bottle.

Ready blobs combine when a player-dragged blob touches a matching value, including throughout its return journey. Actual outline checks cover both directions, edge brushes, and fast swipes; a brief contact is retained across physics substeps. Unmatched contacts do not prevent a later match. Matching neighbors automatically combine and cascade after every shot, merge, rearrangement, swap, or restored save, so the middle differs from both sides once the row is ready. Equal outer values stay separate while the middle differs; shooting the middle makes those two values neighbors and they combine. Refill continues until three values are ready. At 4096, matching capped values occupy opposite sides of a different middle value. Preparation merges do not add points.

The **Shoot** button stays active throughout gameplay, including while blobs shift, return, or refill: hold to choose force, then release to launch the selected blob from where it currently sits in the preparation row (left, middle, or right). The launch point is kept above the opening, within x 94–266 and from the resting row at y 58 down to y 68 at full force. The aiming guide starts at the selected blob’s current outline and follows its motion as it returns to its place. A quick press is gentle; charging for 1.1 seconds reaches full force. While holding, slide left or right to turn the aiming arrow (110 px of travel reaches the widest angle). Sliding up or down by more than 8 px stops the timed charge and sets force directly from the current value: up adds force and down removes it, with 120 px covering the full range. Releasing at 0% after sliding cancels; a quick tap still fires gently. The larger Shoot button sits between ‹ and › buttons that select the ready blob to the left or right of the current one. Alternatively, start a drag inside the bottle to aim, move farther to increase force, and release to shoot; move above the glass to cancel. Keyboard: 1/2/3 select a blob, left/right aim, up/down choose force, and Space shoots. Every aiming method shares the same per-position limit: from the middle it is 55° either side of straight down; from a side slot, aiming toward the far side still allows 55°, but aiming toward the nearer rim narrows smoothly to 48° at 20 units from the center, 41° at 40, 26° at 63 and 14° at the outermost slot (86 units). A charging blob sinks up to 10 units below the row at full force, and the launch point follows it only in proportion to force. The limits are checked with the actual particle physics in an empty bottle, including stiff, highly viscous, small and large blobs. Launches cannot start above the resting row: a raised, fully stiff 2048 blob could ricochet out. Run `node tests/simulate-slot-limits.mjs` for the reproducible 2048/4096 edge sweep. Collisions with an existing pile can still cause normal spills.

Launches and side arrivals begin as area-preserving teardrop membranes, with a broad leading body and narrow trailing tip aligned to motion. Launch force and stiffness affect stretch; a small differential release velocity produces strain while preserving mean launch momentum. A transient rest-shape deformation relaxes with the chosen material, and the same particles respond to glass and other blobs. Full-power shots reach 1080 world units per second, 20% above the earlier 900. Larger board blobs use reduced relative inertia (effective mass grows with radius to the 1.6 power), and substantial glass/blob impacts retain 25% normal rebound. Low-speed contacts settle without added bounce. Preparation blobs retain their controlled contact response for dragging and placement. This is a stylized soft-body approximation, not a fluid dynamics solver.

Incoming values are uniformly random 2, 4, 8, 16, or 32, arriving from a random left or right side. Arrival impacts transfer momentum and deform neighboring membranes. Merge growth waits until source blobs touch; sources that start apart stretch toward each other while a gentler spring draws them together. On contact, a soft ring in the result’s color expands and fades over 0.45 s, 14 small sparks (result color and cream) burst from its edge for 0.55 s, and the merged blob flashes briefly. It then does a 0.5 s jelly pop: it starts stretched along the merge direction at 65% size, overshoots to about 115% at 0.12 s, and wobbles back to exactly full size. Grabbing, shooting, or refilling mid-pop snaps the result to full size and opacity. With reduced motion, preparation merges resolve without rings, sparks, or overshoot.

Bottle merges are not instant. When two matching blobs first touch, they fuse for 0.3 s: they stop pushing each other apart and are pulled together while still colliding normally with the glass and every other blob. The merged blob then appears at their midpoint, stretched along the pair, and grows from its sources’ size to full size over 0.3 s. Score is added once, when the merged blob appears. A fusing pair and a growing result are saved and resume mid-animation. With a reduced-motion preference, matching blobs merge on first contact at full size. All three ready choices remain controllable during preparation motion. Grabbing a blob retargets existing motion from its current pose instead of waiting or snapping it home. Shoot takes priority over an active preparation drag and accepts manual shots during refill/cooldown; stale events from the earlier gesture cannot fire again. Normal uninterrupted merge/arrival animations still play, and reduced-motion preferences shorten transitions. There is no incoming preview in the controls. Existing saves and settings are preserved.

The 360×420 playfield uses a shorter, wider translucent glass bottle. Its open lip curves inward to a narrower waist, then outward through rounded shoulders into the broad body and rounded base. Older settled piles move upward with the shorter base while preserving shape, score, and velocity. Particle collisions use the same outline that is drawn. Shots can bounce from the rim and neck, and blobs follow the shoulder walls into the wider body. A launched blob ends the round only once its entire membrane clears the bottle interior. A center outside, protruding tip, or half-out body stays playable. Below the opening, a membrane cannot straddle the glass: if particles on both sides are joined by an edge, the whole body joins the side that holds most of it. A blob that clips the lip and slides down the outside therefore spills instead of being pulled back inside. The dotted line is a capacity warning rather than a loss timer. Once fully outside, the blob continues falling visibly along the bottle exterior before the spill result appears. Normal contact with either side of the glass remains a collision. Final separation passes resolve membrane contact, followed by a convex separation correction only for remaining actual intersections. This handles nested bodies and crowded merge results without replacing their soft deformation. A held blob is kinematic so neighbors yield. New merge results inherit the interior side of the glass before their enlarged outline meets the floor. Glass-side ownership changes only along a recorded path through the open mouth; collision-adjusted Verlet velocity is never treated as a crossing. A previously saved blob stuck below the base is recovered even when its center is outside, while genuine exterior spills retain their outside state. Faces and highlights are clipped to each blob’s rendered membrane, preventing them from painting through neighboring blobs or the base. Merge playback replaces the source faces with one growing result instead of layering all of them. Fresh shots above the opening are allowed, and preparation drags cannot trigger this rule. The resting preparation cluster stays above the opening. Dragged blobs remain outside the glass and return around its exterior.

Open the sliders icon in the header for **Physics settings**. Stiffness (0–100%) spans liquid-like jelly to almost rigid blobs, with a much stronger response near both endpoints. Viscosity (0–100%) controls motion drag; higher values make movement slower and more damped. Blob size (40–150%) changes real collision and rendered geometry, including existing blobs and the preparation row. Resizing preserves the pile, score, and velocities; collisions reconcile the resized pile when play resumes. A live preview and Reset to defaults help compare the feel. Defaults are 10% stiffness, 0% viscosity, and 150% size. The old stock 30%/20%/80% profile upgrades once on reload; custom preferences stay unchanged, including a later deliberate choice of that old profile. Reset to defaults restores 10%/0%/150%. Settings save automatically in this browser and carry into new rounds. The main game pauses while a dialog is open.

Includes local save/resume, personal best, pause/restart, bottom left/right selection, sound toggle, accessible controls, reduced particle effects for reduced-motion preferences, responsive phone layout, and a home-screen manifest. Active play has no score cards or floating point labels. Round score and personal best appear only in pause, saved-resume, and game-over summaries. Touch-action settings and a non-passive capture-phase touch-end guard on Shoot suppress native rapid-tap zoom. Shoot stays natively enabled; paused, modal and ended states are guarded in code and exposed with ARIA. The viewport does not disable user scaling elsewhere. Blobs use deformable 24-particle membranes with edge elasticity, area pressure, and collisions against the actual outlines. They flatten on impact, wobble, and conform to neighboring blobs. Existing saves are migrated automatically. Browser storage is specific to the device, browser, and site origin. There is no cloud sync.

Serve `dist/` with any static HTTP server. No build or dependency installation is needed.

```sh
npm start
npm test
```

On iPhone, open the deployed link in Safari. Use Share → Add to Home Screen for a standalone view. No signed iPhone installation is needed for this version.

The separate native Swift project remains in the parent directory for later iPhone development.
